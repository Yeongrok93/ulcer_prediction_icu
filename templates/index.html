ICU Pressure Injury Risk Calculator

Nurse-centered machine learning model (Gaussian Naïve Bayes) — research prototype

Research prototype. This tool characterizes cumulative pressure-injury risk patterns observed during an ICU stay. It is not a validated real-time bedside predictor and must not replace clinical judgment or standard risk assessment.

How to use
  1. All inputs are cumulative summaries of the ICU stay so far (mean RASS, mean lower-limb strength, lowest and highest temperature, total incontinence episodes), not values from a single shift. Enter them together with the number of ICU days.
  2. Daily incontinence is computed automatically as total episodes ÷ ICU days (stays shorter than one day are treated as one day).
  3. Click Calculate risk to obtain the probability and the contributing factors.
  4. Nothing you enter is stored on the server.
FAQ
How is the probability calculated?+
A Gaussian Naïve Bayes classifier was trained on retrospective ICU records. The model multiplies, for each variable, how likely the entered value is among patients who developed a pressure injury versus those who did not. The raw output is then recalibrated with isotonic regression so that the displayed percentage reflects observed event rates in the development data.
Why these five variables?+
They are routinely documented by ICU nurses and are directly linked to preventive nursing actions: repositioning and mobilization (muscle strength, consciousness), temperature management (minimum and maximum temperature), and incontinence care.
How accurate is the model?+
In internal validation on a held-out test set (30% of 23,443 adult ICU admissions from a single tertiary hospital), the area under the ROC curve was 0.90 with sensitivity 0.83 and specificity 0.85 at the default 50% threshold. Performance has not been externally validated and may differ in other settings.
What period should the inputs cover?+
The model was developed with variables aggregated over the ICU stay (up to pressure-injury onset for patients who developed one). It therefore reflects cumulative exposure during the stay rather than a single point in time; follow your unit's guidance on the observation window.
Does the model update with my inputs?+
No. The model is fixed at the version trained for this study; entered values are not stored or used for retraining.
